# Living Millhaven: purposeful downtime and visiting trade

**Status:** opt-in implementation available for newly generated scenarios (`--purposeful-downtime`); existing saved episodes keep their frozen scenario and policy. This document also records remaining aspirational criteria, not guarantees of end-to-end day-one recovery.

## Problem and observed behavior

In the inspected day-one episode, Mara spent 375 minutes waiting, Hobb 380, and Finn 645. The scenario enables event-based waits but sets `anti_idle=false`. Even when enabled, the existing anti-idle fallback primarily suggests eligible local occupation work, due meals, maintenance, and a bounded workplace inspection; it does not plan noticeboard visits, social activities, new crafts, or neighbor assistance. Its `idle_review_minutes` default is 60. A `village_wait_event` can therefore reserve a resident until a known event or safety review; with anti-idle off it may reserve much longer. Posting a paper note does not notify an absent recipient.

In that episode a fish request for Finn was posted on the square board at 07:05; Finn started a wait at the riverbank at 09:35 without learning of it. Torvin's timeline also displayed overlapping event-wait blocks: waits started at 19:15, 19:35, 19:40, 20:15 and 20:25, each retaining a 22:00 reservation even after he was woken and acted again. This is a timeline projection issue: a wake shortens the *actual* availability, but an older action-result observation still holds its original end time. The UI should truncate an earlier wait when the next recorded action starts (see [world timeline](ui/src/world-gantt.ts)); the downtime policy should separately prevent needless repeated event waits. Mara had learned of a flour request, but the mill was broken. Hobb completed the two-plank production target; his timber request to Rowan was rejected. Existing hobbies include Hobb's woodcraft reading/practice and Finn's fishing survey and river rest; Finn performed the survey and rest before waiting. None of these hobbies constitutes physical furniture, preserved fish, or saleable tackle.

Iona spent substantial time on herbal research and visited the woods at 17:10 to observe a plant for her herbal book; she completed notes, drafting and revision afterward. She did **not** gather herbs or tend a garden in the observed episode. The existing `village_gather_herbs` action is tied to her herbalism skill. The scenario also has a `garden-tending` activity with a **gardening** skill requirement and one real manure input, but its implementation additionally requires a resident with **farming** skill; Iona's configured body/roster currently lists herbalism, and her configured interests list plants and music, not gardening. Thus her gardening aptitude is not represented as an eligible garden activity. The farm's hens can produce manure after actual care, and farm/stable manure stocks exist, but no dedicated herbal plot or authorized Iona manure route is configured. Her research and woodland observation should not be mistaken for physical gardening or herb harvesting.

The **iron trader did visit** the square from 12:00 to 18:00 and left with two unsold iron units. The *separate* travelling merchant/visitor event did not occur: the current event configuration sets `daily_min=0`, `daily_max=2`; its seeded day-one random target was **zero**. The visiting-merchant template is random, not scheduled. Merchant events must also pass the event safety checks and active-event limits. The existing merchant buys planks, wagon parts, tea and bread, not furniture, tackle or preserved fish.

## Additional findings from the completed day-one run

The latest Living Millhaven run reached midnight at minute 1440 and paused, **not** a successful recovery. The end-of-day verifier recorded a working wagon, dug well and completed animal care, but an unrepaired mill, no resumed bread supply, an unfilled general-store grain reserve, and only Ada fed at supper (11 of 12 residents missed it). These are additional next-cycle problems, not merely cosmetic waits:

1. **Missed repair trigger and crew rendezvous (highest priority).** Hobb collected the replacement beam at 09:20, but attempts to repair the mill at 10:10, 11:30 and 12:35 correctly failed because the drive had **not yet broken**. At 13:15 he started an event wait at the workshop until 19:00; the drive actually failed at 14:00, and a bell event was emitted **at the mill**, asking Hobb and Mara to meet there. With physical communication enabled, its witnesses were local to the mill; the workshop did **not** automatically hear it. Hobb never came back before bedtime. Mara waited at the broken mill from 14:10 through 17:00, expecting him. Add an explicit, physically grounded relay or bounded scheduled check-in to communicate the incident to Hobb, wake his event wait when he personally learns it, then plan travel and a crew rendezvous. Do not treat a mill-local announcement as audible at the workshop, repair before breakdown, or infer private information from remote events. Escalate a stalled critical repair when one worker is missing, rather than silently waiting through its whole work window.
2. **Supper and food-chain planning (highest priority).** Elsie prepared only four meals at 07:30; at 18:30 she reported one remaining portion reserved for Ada and no tavern bread. Bakery still had four bread at midnight, while the tavern had none. The mill broke at 14:00; Jory's flour procurement/production was blocked by uncommitted inputs and stale physical messages. Provide an opt-in, deadline-aware *village meal forecast* with real bread/meal stocks, paid allocations, feasible batch and route capacity, and time to 19:00. Prioritize a physical bakery-to-tavern delivery or other authorized real food source before speculative projects; never conjure meals, raid reserved stock, or bypass prices. Warn while supper is still recoverable, then identify the specific missing ingredient, driver, worker or route rather than merely repeating a wait.
3. **Accepted supply orders without a feasible fulfillment path.** Elsie's accepted two-milk order from Ada failed at its 18:30 deadline with zero delivered. Animal care completed at 14:45, but Ada's milking attempts later returned `DEMAND_MET` even though the order was already `blocked` with `remaining=2`, `commitment=0`, and `reason='Planned production did not complete'`; her attempt at 17:50 exceeded the daytime collection window, and an egg-request acceptance was declined for lack of carrying capacity or delivery route. Have the order planner distinguish *accepted incoming demand* from reserve-covered stock and blocked orders needing replanning, account for equipment/space, collection duration, transit and buyer receipt before a deadline, and surface a concrete blocker to both parties. Replan on accepted orders, care completion, inventory/route changes and approaching expiry; do not make a commitment appear feasible merely because an ingredient exists somewhere.
4. **Unproductive communication and travel loops.** The run recorded 49 `COMMUNICATION_INVALID` and 19 `CHANNEL_UNAVAILABLE` rejections, including repeated checks of unchanged paper, wrong-site replies and repeated production-plan attempts with unchanged inputs. Time-use recorded 145 minutes of wasted travel for Torvin and 125 for Nella. Preserve physical, per-person knowledge but expose the required channel, route, next valid check, funding/stock/skill/capacity blocker and stale version in the *actor's own* observation after a failed attempt. Cache unchanged blockers until a relevant event changes them; let the planner batch eligible errands and avoid repeated speculative visits. Count rejected and no-change actions separately from genuine work in diagnostics.
5. **Overoptimistic recoverability reporting.** Late supervisor reviews continued to describe the day's requirements as recoverable even after the supper window had passed and the mill team was idle. At midnight the recorded verifier still reported `daily_requirements_recoverable=true` despite the missed supper, broken mill, failed milk order and grain shortage. Separate next-day repairability from *today's deadline feasibility*; show irreversible daily misses as soon as their deadline passes, give the supervisor an explicit blocked/missed-goal signal with evidence, and retain the saved world's authoritative outcomes. Do not declare success or continue to promise same-day completion from an impossible schedule.

These findings extend, rather than replace, the existing anti-idle and merchant scope. The late-run counts are a snapshot of the completed run and should become regression fixtures, not global thresholds.

## Desired outcomes

1. With an explicit purposeful-downtime policy enabled, a resident who lacks immediately feasible priority work checks personally accessible information, seeks useful local work, and chooses bounded interests or rest rather than entering an all-day wait. No hidden or remote information is revealed.
2. New optional activities have real sites, tools, resources, duration and consequences. Goods intended for sale exist as accountable stock; recreation cannot silently create stock or coin.
3. Residents may help with suitable outstanding tasks by agreement or clear public assignment without displacing owners, accepting commitments for others, or inventing skills and materials.
4. Visiting trade is observable and configurable: guaranteed scheduled opportunities can be opted into without converting all visits into guaranteed purchases. The iron-trader import and visitor-merchant systems remain distinct.

## Policy and planning

Add a scenario/runtime policy such as `purposeful_downtime` (default **off** for existing scenarios), gated by timed AI-controlled daily-life rules and compatible with `anti_idle`. If both are enabled, extend the existing fallback planner rather than creating a second independent scheduler. Preserve scenario snapshots, paused-world configuration semantics and replay/recovery. Expose configured thresholds in the day policy and observations.

At each free turn, select the first **feasible** category, considering the actor's current location, personally known facts, necessary travel, available time, priorities and validated engine actions:

1. Due food/rest, accepted orders, repair/project appointments, animal care, assigned maintenance and actionable incidents.
2. Eligible occupation production and research where real demand, equipment, inputs, reserves and work windows permit.
3. Personally known, outstanding requests; read a *local* workplace inbox or board only if it has an unread accessible paper version. If there is no feasible nearby task, allow a planned trip to the square board at a bounded interval (proposed maximum 90 minutes of otherwise free daytime) and read it there. The departure decision uses a scheduled information-check need, **not** omniscient knowledge of remote postings. Recheck after a known deadline or interruption. Reading an old paper version does not imply knowledge of a later expiry; verify terms and status through supported channels before accepting or producing for a commitment.
4. Eligible neighbor assistance and optional projects/trade preparation, then a personally preferred feasible hobby. Choose variety with per-activity cooldowns and daily caps; do not loop through reading, inspections, no-op research or resource-consuming practice for time-use credit.
5. A recorded event wait only if no feasible alternative exists. Bound the next safety review to at most 30 minutes during the active workday (configurable in 5-minute increments); break earlier for a locally witnessed event, personally learned message, feasible work becoming available or obligations. Waiting must not invoke the model on every scheduler tick or occupy other villagers' turns.

Account for walking plus task duration before supper and bedtime; do not start a batch that cannot finish safely. Record candidate, rejection reason, selected action and next review in personal observations and the time-use timeline. Do not autonomously travel to a private inbox, claim another resident's knowledge, or repeatedly re-read unchanged paper.

Register new goods and recipes in the village's inventory, stock-lot, provenance, spoilage, research and economy systems, and add physical fixtures only when the scenario opts in. Specify prices and trader demand explicitly. Merchants may buy furniture, tackle or preserved fish only if offered by their profile, locally present, solvent and within cargo capacity; reject exports that violate reserves, accepted orders, or required remaining freshness through journey and resale. No automatic sale or fabricated foreign buyer. Maintain supply-chain conservation and idempotent transfers on retry/load.

## Helping neighbors

Offer a discoverable help request or explicit local cooperation invitation for real, unfinished work. The helper must personally learn it via board, inbox, conversation or direct observation and have the necessary capability, access and equipment. Eligible examples include carrying a funded delivery, gathering materials under a valid request, or contributing to a repair at the worksite; do not allow a helper to perform an occupation-only action, spend another resident's funds or override assigned maintenance. Require owner/crew permission for privately owned resources, track who contributed and how much, settle any agreed payment once, and prevent duplicate completion. Protect rest, meals, the helper's existing obligations and the recipient's capacity for coordinated work.

## External visitor availability

Keep the daily noon iron importer separate from event-system visitors. Expose both in a human-readable dashboard with location, arrival/departure and any reason a pending visit is skipped. For opt-in, predictable export opportunities, allow a **scheduled** merchant visit on chosen days in a specified daytime window, with a finite purse, inventory, product interests and departure time. The event activator must reserve or otherwise account for scheduled visits separately from random daily targets so a zero random draw cannot suppress a promised visit. If safety/capacity prevents activation, record a retry window and explicit missed/expired reason; do not teleport a visitor or silently fabricate a sale. Leave occasional random travellers/merchants probabilistic under the existing seeded configuration. Scheduled supply is not guaranteed demand or guaranteed purchases.

An opt-in scheduled merchant arrives or reports a concrete blocker/missed visit; save/reload does not duplicate the visitor.
- With purposeful downtime on, Finn at the riverbank without known work plans a bounded *physical* board visit; only reading the posted fish note gives him knowledge. If that paper is already expired in the authoritative ledger, acceptance is refused/updated instead of generating an invalid delivery. - With a broken mill, Mara is not assigned impossible grinding; with the workshop target met, Hobb is not assigned unnecessary planks. Both may select feasible help, research or hobbies before a short event wait. Verify obligation-aware travel and priority ordering.
- Recreational release creates no fish. Tackle/furniture/smoked fish consume real inputs, produce accounted output once, respect ownership/reserves/accepted orders and survive save/recovery. Ineligible skills, absent tools/fuel, stale fish and insufficient merchant funds/capacity are rejected.
- Iona's existing woods observation for her book remains distinct from herb harvesting; garden care requires the new herb plot with represented gardening aptitude and herbalism eligibility, not Ada's farming-only activity. Test hen care produces a finite manure lot at the farm, a permitted physical transfer to her plot consumes available unreserved manure without duplicating it or granting Iona animal-care powers, and garden tending/fertilizing consumes that input. Also test a real trip to the woods, bounded survey/harvest, finite plot yield, no herb stock on observation or tending alone, and prioritization of her existing herb/tea commitments, meals and bedtime.
- No resident repeatedly polls an unchanged board/inbox or performs an unlimited loop of low-value hobbies. Track daytime wait minutes and reason-coded inactivity in time-use metrics; a long wait is permitted only with a documented lack of feasible alternatives. In the Gantt view, interrupted Torvin-style event waits end at the next recorded action and never overlap their replacements, while uninterrupted waits retain their original duration.
- Replay the completed day-one run as a regression fixture: a 13:15 workshop event wait is interrupted only after Hobb receives a physical relay or learns of the 14:00 mill breakdown through a scheduled check-in, and Hobb and Mara meet and perform the repair only after the actual failure with their real beam, plank and tools. If the repair cannot be completed, both receive a reasoned blocker and Mara is not left polling at the watermill until evening.
- With a 19:00 supper requirement for twelve, planning accounts for actual committed portions and bakery-to-tavern lead time; expose a shortage early enough for feasible resupply or an honest unavoidable-failure report. An accepted milk order counts toward demand only when collection and delivery can finish by deadline; a covered-reserve response cannot silently strand the order. Milk/eggs require real capacity, equipment and authorized routes.
- Repeated wrong-channel or unchanged-condition actions result in a concrete personal blocker and do not produce a loop of identical rejections. An end-of-day verifier reports missed supper, unfulfilled order, broken mill and grain shortage independently of whether work remains possible tomorrow; the supervisor does not call an already missed same-day requirement recoverable.

## Implementation status and remaining limits

The opt-in scenario generator installs short daytime reviews, physical noticeboard checks and mill coordination, finite crafts and gardening, bread-delivery suggestions for personally known orders, blocked-order replanning, and a scheduled visiting merchant. Deadline reporting distinguishes missed supper from next-day repairability. The Gantt projection truncates interrupted waits. These changes do not retroactively replay the inspected run or guarantee its full recovery: residents still have to execute suggested travel, repair and deliveries, personally learn orders, and have feasible supplies and funds. The existing-neighbor help event is available, but a general cross-occupation assistance commitment system is not implemented. Garden manure permission is not separately recorded, the speculative stock cap is fixed, and a full scripted end-to-end replay of the failed day remains outstanding.

2. Add neighbor-help commitments and bounded hobby selection using existing social/research guards.
3. Add real craft recipes/goods, smoker/fuel/freshness rules and merchant offers with conservation/recovery tests.
4. Add scheduled merchant activation and skip diagnostics; verify independent iron trader behavior and regression suites.
5. Add physically relayed incident rendezvous, deadline-aware meals and accepted-order replanning; verify food/repair fixture and both success and honestly missed-goal paths. Add actor-visible action blockers and distinguish same-day recoverability from future repairability.
